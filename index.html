Great Teachers of India

Indian Monks and Spiritual Leaders

Across India's diverse spiritual traditions, teachers have encouraged compassion, discipline, self-knowledge and service to others.

Swami Vivekananda

A Hindu monk and teacher who inspired young people to develop strength, confidence, service and fearlessness. His message encourages us to believe in our potential and help others.

Life lesson: Believe in yourself and serve others.

Gautama Buddha

The founder of Buddhism taught compassion, mindfulness, moderation and freedom from hatred. His teachings encourage us to understand suffering and respond with wisdom rather than anger.

Life lesson: Pause, understand and act with compassion.

Adi Shankaracharya

The philosopher and spiritual teacher is associated with Advaita Vedanta, which explores the unity of ultimate reality and the self. His tradition emphasizes inquiry, discernment and spiritual understanding.

Life lesson: Seek knowledge and think deeply.
Pride of Karnataka

Saints and Vachana Poets of Karnataka

Karnataka's spiritual heritage includes the Vachana movement, devotional poetry and teachers who reflected on equality, honest work, devotion and inner transformation.

Basavanna

The 12th-century Karnataka saint, poet and social reformer is associated with the ideals of Kayaka (dignity of honest work) and Dasoha (sharing and service). He challenged social discrimination and emphasized ethical living.

Values: Equality, honest work, generosity.

Akka Mahadevi

A celebrated Kannada Vachana poet and spiritual seeker, Akka Mahadevi expressed deep devotion, independence of thought and a commitment to her spiritual path. Her poetry invites reflection on inner freedom and sincerity.

Values: Courage, devotion, authenticity.

Allama Prabhu

A mystical Kannada saint-poet associated with the Vachana tradition. His writings question superficial appearances and encourage spiritual inquiry, self-reflection and freedom from attachment.

Values: Wisdom, simplicity, self-awareness.
Values for Everyday Life

Moral Values We Can Practise

Wisdom becomes meaningful when we practise it at home, in school, at work and in society.

Truthfulness

Speak truthfully, admit mistakes and earn trust through honest actions.

Compassion

Understand other people's feelings and help without expecting a reward.

Equality

Respect every person regardless of background, status or ability.

Dignity of Work

Do your work sincerely. Every honest contribution has value.

Self-Discipline

Manage your time, control harmful impulses and fulfil your responsibilities.

Sharing and Service

Share your knowledge, support people in need and contribute to your community.

The real measure of wisdom is how kindly and honestly we treat others.
